## 1. The series

### 1.1 Name and theme

**Series 4 "Moonwick".** The name joins the moon (night magic) and the wick (the fuse, the game's clock). Moonwick is a tall tower
of spell-casters lit by candles. Each item is one of its rooms and the enchantment cast in it. The series has a shared world
and a shared alphabet, and every item keeps its own material, palette and motion:

| Room (floor) | Skin | Pop | Board | Fuse | Set |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Library | Spellbook (R) | Paper Birds (R) | Grand Library (C) | Candlewick (C) | Library set |
| The Moon Garden | Glowcap (C) | Fireflies (C) | Moonlit Glade (R) | Star Wand (R) | Glade set |
| The Alchemy Lab | Potion (E) | Gold Leaf (E) | Alchemy Lab (E) | Elixir (E) | Alchemy set |
| The Time Vault | Hourglass (L) | Sandfall (E) | Observatory (L) | Sandglass (L) | Time Vault set |
| The Spire | Sigil (M) | Constellation (M) | Moonwick Tower (M) | Moonrise (M) | Spire set |
| Rooms on their own | Patchwork (C), Origami (R), Gemcut (E) | Wishdust (C), Rune Burst (R), Whirlgate (L) | Floating Isles (L) | Runechain (L) | |
| Pop Pass, Season 4 | Moonglass (M, tier 30) | Wisps (E, tier 25) | | | |

The sets go up in rarity, so they also set the pace. The Library and the Glade are made of Commons and Rares, so a player completes
one within the first hours. Alchemy is all Epic, the Time Vault is mostly Legendary, and the Spire is the four Mythics.

Two findings from the review that shaped the rules below:
- **Purple is crowded.** Six items already use space violet. No Series 4 item uses violet as its main field (the Sigil and Potion
  skins show violet only on Amethyst bricks).
- **Sapphire and Amethyst merge for colour-blind players on every skin that ships.** Measured on the run screen with simulated
  deuteranopia (section 6.4), the smallest colour difference between a Sapphire and an Amethyst brick is 0.3 to 5.2 Delta E on the
  current skins (Classic 3.5), which is nearly invisible. The game has a Shape hints setting, but it is off by default. Series 4
  draws a shape into the art itself.

### 1.3 Series rules (on top of the Update 3 coherence rules in UPDATE-3.md 2.4)

1. **The Seven Sigils.** Ruby = circle (Sun), Sapphire = diamond (Tide), Emerald = triangle (Grove), Topaz = square (Gate),
   Amethyst = hexagon (Crystal), Amber = star (Star), Aqua = cross (Wind). These are the exact outlines of the existing Shape hints
   (`css/run.css`), with an inner mark added. Wherever a Series 4 item draws a symbol, it draws the colour's sigil: embroidered
   (Patchwork), embossed (Spellbook), as a fold (Origami), a bottle shape (Potion), a gem cut (Gemcut), etched (Moonglass), lit
   (Sigil), flying (Rune Burst). With Shape hints on, the hint and the sigil are the same shape, so they never disagree.
2. **Moonlight and candle gold.** Each item has its own main palette (named in its entry; no two Series 4 items share one) plus
   the two series accents: moon silver `#e8f0ff` and candle gold `#ffcf6b`. Badges of Series 4 items get a thin silver inner rim and a small
   sigil mark in the corner, so they read as one series in the Collection.
3. **Cast, then release.** Series 4 moments start with a short anticipation (120 to 200 ms: a ring, glyph or line draws in), then
   release. Series 3 moments burst straight away, so the two series feel different.
4. **The run shows in the scenery.** Where it fits, a Series 4 item records the run in its art: a window per stage (Moonwick
   Tower), a notch per stage (Observatory), a rune per stage (Runechain), a moon phase per stage (Moonglass). These are cosmetic,
   reset with each run, and never replace the HUD.

Why this split:
- **28 is the size of Series 3**, so the build and review effort is already known. Section 3 adds work per item, mostly the
  sigils and moments, so this is not a smaller build than Series 3.
- **Fewer skins and pops than Series 3 (8 instead of 10), more boards and fuses (6 instead of 4).** The Brick and Pop chests are
  already deep (19 and 16). The Board and Fuse chests are the ones players finish and abandon, so they get the most new items, and
  each gets a second Mythic. After Series 4, every chest has between 16 and 27 items and at least 2 Mythics.
- **Rarity mix: 16 of 28 are Epic or better** (Series 3: 16 of 28). The no-duplicate rule means rarity is a pacing tool and a
  quality promise, not a lottery: every Epic and up gets a signature moment, and Mythics arrive last. A player who owns the whole
  old catalogue gets only Series 4 items from every chest. The renormalised odds hand them the new Commons and Rares first and the
  Mythics last, with the pity chest every 8th.
- **Two Pass exclusives** (as on today's Pass, which has Chrome and Prism Heart): an Epic at tier 25 and a Mythic at tier 30, so every player can earn one Series 4
  Mythic without luck.

### 2.3 Economy: time to own everything

Bot results from `node tests/sim.js` (full mode, this build): Coins per run 26 / 54 / 65 and run length 149 / 143 / 145 s for
casual / average / sharp. Missions add 20 Coins per run. The calculation is the one in `tests/sim.js`, reproduced in
`prototypes/econ.py`.

| Case | Coins to spend | Casual | Average | Sharp | A chest every (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today: 56 chest items | 19,400 | 16.2 h | 9.7 h | 8.6 h | 15 min |
| After Series 4, today's prices (84 items) | 29,100 | **27.7 h** | **16.7 h** | **14.7 h** | 15 min |
| ... plus 6 new trophies (+1,200 Coins once) | 29,100 | 26.3 h | 15.9 h | 13.9 h | 15 min |
| Returning player who owns the 56: Series 4 only | 9,700 | 11.6 h | 7.0 h | 6.1 h | 15 min |
| ... plus new trophies and a Season 4 Pass track (section 5.4) | 9,700 | 6.2 h | 3.7 h | 3.3 h | |

The total at today's prices: **84 chest items cost 29,100 Coins** (Brick 9,450, Pop 7,200, Board 7,650, Fuse 4,800).

What it means:
- **The pace a player feels does not change.** Coins per run and chest prices stay the same, so an average player still opens a
  chest about every 15 minutes. Only the total grows, by 71 percent of the Coins still to earn.
- **Holding the old 10 hours would mean cutting chest prices to 58 percent or raising Coin income by 71 percent.** Both would
  devalue what players have saved and make the chests feel cheap. With +71 percent run Coins the average would be 10.4 h; with +20
  percent it would be 13.8 h. Neither is recommended.
- **Recommendation: keep prices and per-run Coins.** 16 hours for an average new player to own all 84 items is inside the range
  Update 3 planned for (it targeted 22 hours and shipped 10). Add one-time Coins where they also tell the story:
  - Six trophies, about 1,200 Coins in total: own a Series 4 item of every type (150), complete any Moonwick set (200), complete
    three sets (250), own all four Spire Mythics (300), pop 500 groups wearing a sigil skin (150), own 75 items (150).
  - A real **Season 4 Pop Pass** (section 5.4). For a returning player this makes Series 4 a chase of about 4 hours for an average
    player and 6 for a casual one, instead of 7 and 12: one to two weeks of normal play.
- Optional: the Board Chest is the most expensive per item (450). At 400, the 17 boards cost 850 Coins less, about half an hour
  for an average player.

## 3. Every new item

### 3.0 How they are built, and the budgets every item keeps

**Engines and hooks (all of them exist today):**
- *Skins:* a class in a new `css/s4-skins.css` that styles `.face`, `.stud`, `.deco`, `.sheen` and their pseudo-elements, with
  `--hi`, `--lo`, `--glow` per colour and `--ph` (0 to 6) for per-colour phase. Art that must carry the brick's colour (sigils, gem
  facets, bottle shapes) is baked once per palette colour into one injected stylesheet, the way `js/s3-skins.js` bakes the
  Phoenix and Galaxy SVGs. Moments go in a new `js/s4-skins.js` through `BR.Skins.handlers` (events `pop`, `demo`, `line`,
  `rushStart`, `rushEnd`, `stageUp`) and `BR.Skins.applyHooks`. Short DOM sprites use the existing sprite layer (`#s3fx`, at most
  26 live).
- *Pops:* a recipe in a new `js/s4-pops.js` registered as `BR.PopRecipes[name]`, called once per popped brick with the per-brick
  budget `n`. Particles that move after they are emitted are steered with `BR.U4.steer` (one shared loop, real elapsed time, so
  60 Hz and 120 Hz behave the same). Pop moments are handlers that receive the group's cells. Sound is a `FLAVOR[recipe]` entry in
  `js/audio.js`. Badge art is a `POP[recipe]` entry in `js/badges.js`.
- *Boards:* `--a-bg` (the whole static scene, also used by badges, the reveal and low-end phones), `--a-bg-run` plus `.bg::before`,
  `.bg::after`, `.scene-fx` and `.scene-fx.b` for the moving parts, `--a-board`, `--a-frame`, `--a-glow`, and `beam` for the Line
  Blast beam colour. This goes in a new `css/s4-scenes.css`, with moments in a new `js/s4-scenes.js`.
- *Fuses:* a token block in `js/fuse-art.js` (rope base, low and crit textures, flame shape and colours, ember, ash, heat, smoke,
  special particle, bomb body and face). The shared engine `js/fuse.js` draws all of it. Moments use the same handler route as
  Lightning Rod and Phoenix Tail.

**Small engine additions Series 4 needs (listed once here, referenced by the items):**
1. `js/fx.js`: an `img` particle kind that draws a cached bitmap with rotation and an x/y scale (for paper birds, ghosts, glyphs,
   gold leaf and fireflies), with sprites baked once per palette colour; and `fx.link(x0, y0, x1, y1, color, life)`, a straight line
   that fades (the existing bolt is always jagged).
2. `js/view.js`: `_place()` also writes `--r` and `--c` (row and column) on the brick. Moonglass needs this. It costs two style
   writes per placement and nothing per frame.
3. `js/run.js`: two more `onEvent` calls, `spark` (Last Spark starts) and `sparkEnd` with `{ saved }`. The fuse moments of
   Moonrise, Sandglass and Runechain need them.
4. `js/fuse-art.js` and `js/fuse.js`: flame shape `candle`; special particles `drip`, `twinkle`, `bubble`, `grain`, `chip` and
   `stardust`; six bomb bodies (`wax`, `robe`, `flask`, `hourglass`, `golem`, `moon`); and a body option to leave out its topper
   when Fizz wears a hat (section 4).

**Budgets (the `tests/u4cosmetics.js` rules, extended into a `tests/u5cosmetics.js`):**
- Only `transform` and `opacity` animate. No blend mode on any brick layer, and no layout animation.
- A skin animates at most a third of the board at rest (at most 43 running animations on bricks: one or two layers on every third
  brick, phased by `--ph`). `.face` is never animated, so the group highlight, the hint pulse and the landing squash keep working.
- A board runs at most two continuous animations behind the bricks. Its moments add at most two short ones.
- Pop moments end within 600 ms. Rush-start and stage-up moments end within 900 ms. The stage-up already freezes the fuse for 1.4 s.
- Particles stay inside the per-brick budget `n` (a big pop gets fewer per brick). Moments add at most 40 particles and 3 DOM
  sprites. Low-end phones halve particle counts. The fuse stays inside its pool of 40 particles and its 1.5 ms per frame.
- Low-end and Reduce Motion: no idle animation, no moments, and the original static look (the fuse readout and face still change,
  as today).
- Moments never add another full-screen flash on top of the game's own Line Blast, Rush and stage-up flashes. They draw on the
  board, around it, or in the scene.

**Readability (every item):**
- *Seven colours stay distinct.* A new automated check (built on the readability probe in `prototypes/shoot.js`) measures the
  smallest colour difference between two different brick colours on a seven-colour board, at rest and in Rush. A Series 4 skin must
  score at least 12.5 Delta E for normal vision, which is above the weakest shipped skin measured (Lantern, 11.9 at seven
  colours). Section 6.4 has the measured values.
- *Colour-blind safety.* Seven of the nine Series 4 skins carry the sigil family in their art (stitch, foil, fold, bottle, cut,
  etching, light). The two that do not (Glowcap and Hourglass) keep `.stud` free for the Shape hints, which were checked on the
  Hourglass prototype.
- *The group highlight is never hidden.* No skin uses `.face::before` (the highlight ring) or animates `.face`. Moments that draw
  over the board (the Sigil cast, the Gemcut prism fan, the Whirlgate portal) use lines and keep any fill at 35 percent opacity or
  less. They start on the pop, after the finger is up.
- *The Fuse brick countdown is never hidden.* Every skin selector carries `:not(.fusebrick)`. Nothing bigger than a particle stays
  over the bricks for more than 600 ms: a longer effect is thin line work (Constellation's lines) or sits behind the bricks (as Ink
  Splash does) or outside the board (the Sigil rune frame).
- *HUD text over scenes* passes the Update 4 contrast test (goal band 6:1, score label 3.8:1, score 4.5:1). Floating Isles is the
  first light scene, so it gets a dark scrim behind the HUD (see its entry).

Item ids follow the catalogue: skins `f_`, pops `b_`, boards `a_`, fuses `u_`, all with `series: 4`.

### 3.1 Skins

#### Patchwork (f_patchwork) - Common skin, a room of its own (the apprentices' quilts)
- **Idle look:** a puffy quilted fabric square in the brick's colour. It has a fine woven texture, a soft pillowed bevel, a
  running stitch in cream thread around the edge, and the colour's sigil embroidered in satin stitch in the middle. It is the first
  fabric in the game. Static.
- **A pop:** the equipped Pop plays. The skin adds no moment (Common).
- **Why it stands out:** the coziest board in the game, and a Common that is fully colour-blind friendly.
- **Build:** `.face` texture and stitch border as gradients; `.stud` holds the embroidered sigil (7 baked SVGs with dashed
  strokes). Badge: hero colour Amber, showing the star sigil.
- **Budget:** 0 animations. **Readability:** the colour fills the whole face. The thread is cream with a dark hairline, so it
  reads on Topaz and Aqua as well.

#### Glowcap (f_glowcap) - Common skin, Glade set
- **Idle look:** a glossy toadstool cap in the brick's colour: a wide, flatter dome than Gumdrop, three to five cream spots
  (each colour has its own spot pattern), and a pale gill band at the bottom edge with a thin glow line under the rim, like a
  mushroom lit from below in a night forest. Static.
- **A pop:** the equipped Pop plays.
- **Why it stands out:** the spots and the under-glow make a Common that looks lit, not printed.
- **Build:** CSS gradients only. Badge: hero colour Ruby (the classic red cap).
- **Budget:** 0 animations. **Readability:** the cap is the colour. Shape hints sit over the spots (spots fade to 35 percent in
  Shape hints mode, as Clockwork's art does).

#### Spellbook (f_spellbook) - Rare skin, Library set
- **Idle look:** a small closed book seen from the front. The cover is leather in the brick's colour with a faint grain and a
  darker spine strip on the left. It has gilt corner guards, the colour's sigil embossed in gold foil in the centre, cream page
  edges at the right and bottom, and a ribbon bookmark tail. Static.
- **A pop:** the equipped Pop plays.
- **Why it stands out:** the first leather and gold-leaf material; the board looks like a shelf of spellbooks.
- **Build:** `.face` leather gradient; `.deco` holds the corners and page edges (gradients); `.stud` holds the foil sigil (baked
  SVG). Badge: hero colour Emerald.
- **Budget:** 0 animations. **Readability:** about 85 percent of the face is the colour. The gold is used for small details only.

#### Origami (f_origami) - Rare skin, a room of its own (the paper-charm workshop)
- **Idle look:** folded matte paper, with each colour folded differently along its sigil family: a round rosette fold (Ruby), a
  kite fold (Sapphire), a triangle fold (Emerald), four flaps meeting in the middle (Topaz), a hexagon twist (Amethyst), a star fold
  (Amber) and a pinwheel cross (Aqua). The faces of each fold are flat light and dark facets, drawn with one conic gradient. It is
  the only skin with no gloss at all. Static.
- **A pop:** the equipped Pop plays.
- **Why it stands out:** a completely flat, paper-craft board unlike anything in the collection, and each colour has its own fold.
- **Build:** `.face` conic-gradient facets per `data-c` plus crease lines. No SVG. Badge: hero colour Topaz.
- **Budget:** 0 animations. **Readability:** facets vary lightness by at most 18 percent so the hue stays clear. The fold
  pattern is a second cue for colour-blind players.

#### Potion (f_potion) - Epic skin, Alchemy set
- **Idle look:** a glass bottle filled with the colour's elixir, set on a glass panel tinted in the same colour. The bottle shape
  follows the sigil: round flask, diamond decanter, conical flask, square bottle, hexagonal bottle, star bottle, and a vial with a
  cross stopper. Each has a cork on top, a bright meniscus line and two tiny bubbles. On every third brick the liquid surface tips
  gently (plus or minus 3 degrees) and the bubbles rise.
- **A pop:** the bottle breaks into white glass shards, three to five droplets of the elixir, and a small coloured vapour puff
  that rises.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* a column of coloured smoke rises from the group and blooms into a glittering cloud that drifts up and fades
    (one sprite and 12 sparkles, 600 ms), with a bubbling glug.
  - *Line Blast:* bubbles stream along the blasted line in the line's colour.
  - *Rush:* every potion boils. The bubbles rise twice as fast and the liquid glows brighter (a static brighter gradient under
    `.rushing`).
  - *Stage-up:* the corks pop. On every third bottle the cork jumps up and drops back (300 ms), with a little pop sound.
- **Why it is epic:** the bottle shapes make every colour a different object, and the board reacts to Rush and stage-ups like a
  lab bench.
- **Build:** the bottle silhouettes are baked per colour (SVG with the glass outline and cork) in `.deco`; the liquid is
  `.deco::before` (clip-path bottle interior, rotate animation); the bubbles are `.deco::after` (translateY loop); handlers for
  `pop`, `line`, `rushStart` and `stageUp`. Badge: hero colour Aqua.
- **Budget:** 2 layers on every third brick (43 animations at most); a pop adds at most 6 particles per brick; the big-pop moment
  is 1 sprite and 12 particles; the cork pop is 22 short transforms.
- **Readability:** the tinted glass panel keeps the whole cell in the colour, and the bottle shape is a second cue.

#### Gemcut (f_gemcut) - Epic skin, a room of its own (the Treasury)
- **Idle look:** a faceted gemstone named after its colour (ruby, sapphire, emerald, topaz, amethyst, amber, aquamarine) in a
  thin silver bezel. The cut follows the sigil: round brilliant, diamond cut, trillion, princess square, hexagon cut, star cut and
  a cross-faceted cushion. The facets are conic and linear gradients around a bright table facet. A white four-point glint crosses
  the gem every 6 seconds, on every third brick.
- **A pop:** the gem shatters into facet shards (triangles in the gem's colour and its light tint) with white sparkles and a thin
  refraction ring.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* the prism. A white beam fires up from the group and splits into a fan of seven rays, one in each brick colour,
    that sweeps across the board for 450 ms (one conic-fan sprite, scale and opacity).
  - *Line Blast:* ten glints run along the beam.
  - *Rush:* every gem's glint speeds up, and fine star sparkles drift down over the board.
  - *Stage-up:* one polishing gleam sweeps diagonally across the whole board (one element, translate).
- **Why it is epic:** the palette is already named after jewels and this is where it pays off. Each colour is a different cut, so
  colours read by shape as well.
- **Build:** facets per `data-c` in `.face` and `.stud` (gradients); glint in `.deco::after`; handlers for `pop`, `line`,
  `rushStart` and `stageUp`. Distinct from Prism Heart (smooth pastel crystal with a heart) and Stained Glass (flat glass).
  Badge: hero colour Ruby.
- **Budget:** 1 layer on every third brick; prism fan 1 sprite, 450 ms; Rush sparkles 20 particles.
- **Readability:** facet lightness is kept within plus or minus 20 percent. The cuts are a shape cue.

#### Hourglass (f_hourglass) - Legendary skin, Time Vault set (prototyped, section 6.1)
- **Idle look:** a colour panel in a thin gold frame, holding a brass-and-glass hourglass whose sand is a bright tint of the
  brick's colour. Sand runs from the top bulb to the bottom over 30 seconds and then stops. New bricks fall in full, so older bricks
  show emptier tops, and the board shows its own age. Every third brick runs its sand; the others hold a level set by their colour.
- **A pop:** the hourglass cracks and its sand pours out in a short falling stream (tiny square grains in the sand colour) with a
  brass ring flash.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* the group's sand swirls into a ribbon that trails the time chip into the fuse readout (12 to 20 grains along
    the chip's arc). The fact that pops buy time becomes visible.
  - *Line Blast:* grains rain down along the blasted line.
  - *Rush:* time stops. All sand freezes mid-fall, the glass frosts, the stream turns into hanging grains and the frames turn
    silver-white. When Rush ends the frost cracks off and the sand runs again, with a soft tick.
  - *Stage-up:* every hourglass turns over in a diagonal wave from the top-left corner (26 ms apart, 420 ms each, about 0.8 s for
    the board), inside the stage-up's 1.4 s fuse hold, and the sand resets.
- **Why it is epic:** it is the game's own clock on every brick. No item has made the whole board move as one wave, and the Rush
  freeze shows the Rush rule (time stops) on every brick.
- **Build:** as prototyped. `.deco` holds the glass, caps and posts (one SVG) and the sand stream (a gradient); `.deco::before` and
  `::after` hold the top and bottom sand (`scaleY` from the bottom edge); `.stud` is free for Shape hints. Rush is CSS only
  (`.run.rushing`: `animation-play-state: paused`, a frost layer, a dotted stream). The flip is a `stageUp` handler that calls
  `.animate()` on each brick's `.deco` with a delay of `(r + c) * 26` ms and then restarts the sand. Badge: hero colour Sapphire,
  which gives the most contrast with the gold.
- **Budget:** at rest, 2 layers on every third brick (43 animations, on the limit); in Rush, 0 running animations (cheaper than
  rest). **The one exception to the third-of-the-board rule:** the stage-up flip runs 64 short transform animations at once,
  while the board is frozen. On the Smooth graphics level only every third brick flips; low-end and Reduce Motion reset the sand
  without a flip. The flip must be added to `tests/perfbudget.js` as a stage-up case.
- **Readability (measured):** Delta E 16.1 at rest and 13.0 in Rush on a seven-colour board (Classic 27.0, Lantern 11.9). Shape
  hints show through the glass.

#### Sigil (f_sigil) - Mythic skin, Spire set (mocked, section 6.3)
- **Idle look:** a jewel-dark brick in its colour with a glowing heart, a gold inlay rim, the colour's sigil in white gold in the
  middle (ring, diamond, triangle, square, hexagon, star or cross, each with an inner mark), and a thin ring of rune ticks that turns
  slowly on every third brick.
- **A pop:** each brick's sigil lifts off for 300 ms (a glyph particle) and dissolves into gold motes.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+), the cast:* a spell circle draws itself around the group: an outer rune band, a hexagram, an inner ring and the
    group's sigil in the centre, at most 300 px wide. It scales in from 0.6 while turning 30 degrees (180 ms), holds (160 ms), then a
    pillar of light fires upward from it, and everything fades by 600 ms. A deep bell chime plays.
  - *Line Blast:* eight runes race along the blasted line ahead of the beam, and the board frame flashes gold.
  - *Rush:* the great circle. A board-sized circle turns slowly under the bricks, showing in the gaps between them. Eight runes set
    into the board frame count down the eight seconds, one going dark each second: a second Rush timer that can be read at a glance.
  - *Stage-up:* every sigil flashes in a ripple from the centre (opacity on `.stud`, every third brick, plus one ring).
- **Why it is epic:** every big pop becomes a spell, and the Rush ring is both a show and useful information.
- **Build:** CSS as mocked. The cast is a DOM sprite whose SVG is built once per colour and cached as a data URI. The pillar is one
  div. The rune frame is one SVG added to `#board` at `rushStart` and removed at `rushEnd`; each second one rune changes class
  (opacity transition). The great circle is one element inside the field, behind the bricks, rotating only during Rush. Handlers:
  `pop`, `line`, `rushStart`, `rushEnd`, `stageUp`. Badge: hero colour Amethyst (the hexagon reads best at 56 px).
- **Budget:** 1 layer on every third brick at rest; the cast is 2 sprites, under 600 ms, and at most 24 particles; Rush adds 1
  continuous animation and 8 opacity transitions.
- **Readability (measured):** Delta E 19.5 at rest and 19.1 in Rush on a seven-colour board, the best of the new skins. The
  sigils are colour-blind cues.

### 3.2 Pops

#### Fireflies (b_fireflies) - Common pop, Glade set
- **What a pop does:** each brick releases one to four fireflies: a warm yellow-green core with a halo in the brick's colour.
  They wander on curving paths and drift upward, blinking on and off (their size switches between full and a quarter every 120 to
  260 ms), and live 1.4 to 2 s. One soft glint marks the brick.
- **Sound:** a soft high "pip" with a breathy tail.
- **Why it stands out:** no other pop wanders or blinks. A big pop leaves a little swarm over the board.
- **Build:** recipe `fireflies` with `steer` (turn rate from a smooth random walk, blink timer). Badge: glowing dots in pale
  green and gold.
- **Budget:** at most 4 particles per brick (ceil(n x 0.35)).

#### Wishdust (b_wishdust) - Common pop, a room of its own (the balcony where wishes are made)
- **What a pop does:** a twist of glitter spirals up from each brick in a helix. Six to ten sparkles orbit a rising axis; the
  ones in front are larger and brighter, and the ones behind are smaller. They rise 60 to 120 px and fade. The colours are white,
  pale gold and the brick's light tint.
- **Sound:** a quick rising three-note twinkle (pentatonic, from the existing pop pitch).
- **Why it stands out:** the helix is a new motion; the burst looks round even though it is drawn flat.
- **Build:** recipe `wishdust`, `steer` sets x from `sin(wt + phase)` and size from the cosine for depth. Badge: small stars in a
  spiral.
- **Budget:** at most 1.1 x n particles per brick.

#### Paper Birds (b_birds) - Rare pop, Library set
- **What a pop does:** each brick folds into a paper bird (flat paper in the brick's colour with a white crease) that flaps away
  up and to the side, and drops three cream paper scraps. In a big pop only every second brick sends a bird, at most six per pop,
  and the rest send scraps.
- **Sound:** two quick paper-flutter bursts and a soft pluck.
- **Why it stands out:** the first flapping creature among the pops, and a new material (paper).
- **Build:** `img` particles (a two-frame bird sprite baked per colour; `steer` flaps by switching the y-scale every 70 ms).
  Badge: new shape `bird`.
- **Budget:** at most 1 bird and 3 scraps per brick, at most 6 birds per pop.

#### Rune Burst (b_runes) - Rare pop, a room of its own (the Scriptorium)
- **What a pop does:** the brick's own sigil flies out as two or three glowing glyph strokes in its colour. They spin a little and
  dissolve into motes, and a thin ring in the colour marks the brick.
- **Sound:** a soft glassy "shing" with a slight detune.
- **Why it stands out:** the particles show which colour was popped. It is the first pop that is also a colour-blind cue.
- **Build:** `img` particles (glyph sprites baked per colour, 7 x 2 sizes). Badge: new shape `glyph`.
- **Budget:** at most 3 glyphs and 4 motes per brick.

#### Gold Leaf (b_goldleaf) - Epic pop, Alchemy set
- **What a pop does:** each brick turns to gold for 80 ms (one gold glint the size of the brick) and crumbles into four to seven
  crinkled gold-leaf flakes. The flakes flutter down and flicker between bright and dark gold, as if they were turning over.
- **Sound:** a bright bell "ting" with a metallic shimmer.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* transmutation. An alchemical ring (circle, triangle and square in gold, 150 px) flares over the group, and a
    gold shimmer ring ripples out across the board.
  - *Line Blast:* the beam turns molten gold for this blast (a beam colour override) and flakes fall along the line.
  - *Rush:* every pop adds a second shower of fine gold dust.
  - *Stage-up:* a gold shimmer sweeps across the stage strip (one element, translate).
- **Why it is epic:** it looks expensive, with flakes that catch the light as they fall. It differs from Coins (round coins that
  spin and bounce) in shape, motion and sound.
- **Build:** recipe `goldleaf` (`img` flakes, `steer` for the flicker), handlers for `pop`, `line`, `rushStart` and `stageUp`.
  Badge: new shape `flake`.
- **Budget:** at most 7 flakes and 1 glint per brick; the ring is 1 sprite; the shimmer is 1 element.

#### Sandfall (b_sandfall) - Epic pop, Time Vault set
- **What a pop does:** each brick dissolves into sand. Ten to sixteen fine grains (1.5 to 2.5 px squares in a light tint of the
  colour and a sand tone) pour down in a narrow stream, with a small puff of dust.
- **Sound:** a soft granular "shhh" (filtered noise in grains).
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* the grains gather into an hourglass shape (two cones meeting at the waist) for 300 ms, then pour toward the fuse
    readout, and the time chip lands in a sandy puff.
  - *Line Blast:* sand cascades down the whole line like a waterfall.
  - *Rush:* each pop's grains hang in the air for 200 ms before they fall (time stopped).
  - *Stage-up:* a curtain of grains falls across the board from top to bottom (40 particles).
- **Why it is epic:** a new material and a new motion (pouring), and its Rush beat shows the Rush rule in every pop.
- **Build:** recipe `sandfall` (pixel shards, `steer` for the hang and the pour), handlers for `pop`, `line`, `rushStart` and
  `stageUp`. Badge: grains around an hourglass core.
- **Budget:** at most 1.6 x n grains per brick; at most 40 extra particles in a moment.

#### Whirlgate (b_whirlgate) - Legendary pop, a room of its own (the Gate room)
- **What a pop does:** each brick is pulled into a tiny vortex. Six to nine particles spiral inward (the radius shrinks as the
  angle grows), then there is a pinpoint flash and a small ring. Colours: deep teal and gold, which are not used by any other pop.
- **Sound:** a downward whoosh and a low thump.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* a portal opens on the group: two counter-rotating ring sprites with spiral particles, the bricks streak into
    it, and it snaps shut with a thump. On 12 or more, a large eye blinks once inside the portal before it closes.
  - *Line Blast:* portals open at both ends of the line, and the beam passes from one into the other.
  - *Rush:* a portal ring opens behind the board at Rush start (600 ms), then settles into a faint turning swirl in the board frame
    for the Rush (1 rotate animation).
  - *Stage-up:* a ring closes in to the centre of the board and opens out again.
- **Why it is epic:** the eye is the moment players tell each other about. The spiral motion differs from Nova Burst's straight
  implosion.
- **Build:** recipe `whirlgate` (`steer` spiral), 2 or 3 DOM sprites per moment, handlers for `pop`, `line`, `rushStart`, `rushEnd`
  and `stageUp`. Badge: shape `spiral`, `rings: true`.
- **Budget:** at most 9 particles per brick; the portal is 3 sprites and under 600 ms; the Rush swirl is 1 animation.

#### Constellation (b_constellation) - Mythic pop, Spire set
- **What a pop does:** each popped brick leaves a bright four-point star that twinkles for about 0.9 s. For every pop of 3 or
  more, the group is drawn as a constellation: thin light lines join the stars along the group's own adjacency (only between
  bricks that touched, at most 14 lines). The lines draw in sequence from the tapped brick, 40 ms per link, and fade at 900 ms.
  The exact shape that was popped is drawn in starlight.
- **Sound:** one soft celesta note per link, rising through the pentatonic scale.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* the constellation comes alive. A star creature (a stag, a whale or a fox, chosen by the group's colour, drawn
    as line art with stars at the joints, 160 to 220 px) bounds out of the group and across the board, then dissolves into stars at
    the edge (one sprite on a curved path, 650 ms, like the Phoenix flight).
  - *Line Blast:* a meteor (a bright head and a long tail) streaks along the line.
  - *Rush:* faint constellations fade into the scene above and below the board, and each pop's lines stay twice as long.
  - *Stage-up:* a ring of stars wheels once around the board.
- **Why it is epic:** every pop draws a different picture, the one the player just made. Big pops summon a creature.
- **Build:** recipe `constellation` (stars) and a `pop` handler that walks the group's cells (from the engine's plan) and calls
  `fx.link` per edge on a 40 ms timer; the creatures are three SVG sprites. Badge: stars joined by lines.
- **Budget:** at most 2 particles per brick, at most 14 links, 1 creature sprite.

### 3.3 Boards

A normal pop never moves a board's scene. Boards react to Line Blasts, Rush and stage-ups, so a board's moment never lands on top of
the Pop's own big-pop moment. The one exception is a quiet easter egg on Moonwick Tower.

#### Grand Library (a_library) - Common board, Library set

  candlelight vignette covers the scene. The board is a dark green leather panel with a double gold-tooled border and corner
  flourishes.
- **Motion:** dust motes drifting in the candlelight (`.scene-fx`) and one book floating and bobbing beside the top shelf

- **Line Blast beam:** candle gold `#ffcf6b`. No moment (Common).
- **Why it stands out:** the first warm indoor scene. Walnut and leather are new colours for the game.

#### Moonlit Glade (a_glade) - Rare board, Glade set

#### Alchemy Lab (a_alchemy) - Epic board, Alchemy set
- **Idle look:** shelves of coloured flasks in the top band, copper pipes and an alembic coil arching around the board's upper
  corners, and a large cauldron in the bottom band bubbling green-gold. The palette is copper (`#c46a2f`), emerald (`#3dffa0`) and

  - *Rush:* every flask lights up and the pipes glow (two opacity layers).
  - *Stage-up:* a drop travels along the copper pipe (one sprite with transform keyframes) and lands in the cauldron with a plink.
  - *Big pop:* none, so it does not double up with the Pop's own moment.
- **Line Blast beam:** emerald.
- **Why it is epic:** the room reacts to how the player plays: blasts boil it and Rush lights it up.
- **Budget:** 2 continuous animations and at most 2 short ones per moment.

#### Observatory (a_observatory) - Legendary board, Time Vault set
- **Idle look:** inside a brass dome. The dome's slit is open to the stars in the top band, and a giant brass telescope crosses

- **Motion:** the orrery's planets orbit (one rotate) and the stars drift past the slit (`.scene-fx`).
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Line Blast:* the telescope swings on its mount (rotate, 600 ms) and a shooting star crosses the slit.

  - *Stage-up:* a gold marker moves one notch along the degree scale on the frame. Its position counts this run's stages (cosmetic

- **Line Blast beam:** pale brass `#ffe2a0`.
- **Why it is epic:** a working instrument room rather than a view of space (unlike Orbit), and the frame records the run.

  island. The board is a pale stone platform with vines at the corners and a deep slate panel behind the bricks.

  - *Line Blast:* the waterfalls surge (a bright water layer translates down, 600 ms) and a flock of birds crosses.

- **Readability (risk):** the first light scene. A soft dark scrim behind the HUD (top band) and the deep slate board panel are

#### Moonwick Tower (a_tower) - Mythic board, Spire set

  - *Rush:* moonrise. The moon slides up and grows full, the clouds part, the scene turns silver (one overlay layer) and every

  - *Stage-up:* one more tower window lights for each stage reached this run, with a small flare. The tower shows how far the run

  - *Big pop (12+):* an owl crosses the moon (one silhouette sprite), as an easter egg.
- **Line Blast beam:** moon silver `#e8f0ff`.
- **Why it is epic:** the board tells the story of the run: windows are stages, the bell is blasts, moonrise is Rush.
- **Build note:** with Moonglass (the Pass skin) equipped, the scene's moon dims during Rush so there is only one moon. This is a

- **Budget:** 2 continuous animations; Rush is 3 layer transitions; each window is a static element with one opacity change.

### 3.4 Fuses

On every pop, the time chip flies to the readout as today, and the flame flares with the fuse's own particle (wax drips, twinkles,
bubbles, grains, stone chips or stardust). Fuses leave big pops to the Pop item and act on Rush, stage-ups, Line Blasts and Last
Spark, where Fizz is the focus. All six fuses keep everything the Update 4 engine draws (Fizz's face and expressions, the
ember, ash and heat, the chips, the urgency ramp, Last Spark, frost in Rush) and change only tokens, plus a moment for Epic and up.

#### Candlewick (u_candle) - Common fuse, Library set
- **Look:** a waxed cotton wick sheathed in ivory wax with drips along its lower edge. In the Low state the wax turns amber, and
  in Critical it turns red. The flame is a new `candle` shape (a tall, calm teardrop with a blue base, an orange-gold tongue and a
  white core). Wax drips fall from the rope and set (special particle `drip`). **Fizz** is a round bomb made of candle wax, with
  drips running down from a brass candle-holder cap. When panicked it drips more, because the crack layer is drawn as drips.
- **Moment:** none (Common).
- **Why it stands out:** the most literal fuse in the game: a candle is a timer.

#### Star Wand (u_wand) - Rare fuse, Glade set
- **Look:** a polished dark-wood wand with silver bands every 32 px. In Low the bands glow amber, in Critical red. The flame is the
  existing `star` shape in pale gold-white, so the burning end is a twinkling wand tip, and four-point `twinkle` stars sparkle off
  as it burns. **Fizz** is midnight blue with a pattern of tiny gold stars and crescent moons, like a wizard's robe, and its cap is
  a gold star.
- **Moment:** none (Rare).

#### Elixir (u_elixir) - Epic fuse, Alchemy set
- **Look:** a glass tube filled with bubbling green elixir: liquid with a meniscus highlight, and bubbles rising inside (special
  particle `bubble`). In Low the elixir turns amber, in Critical red with more bubbles. The flame is the `orb` shape in green, like a
  fizzing reaction. **Fizz** is a round-bottom flask with a cork on top. Its face is on the bulb, and the liquid inside stands at 60
  percent.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Rush start:* Fizz drinks the elixir. The cork pops off (sprite), the liquid level drops, and Fizz glows green-gold and puffs
    up (a 1.15 scale bounce) with sparkles. The tube's liquid freezes into green crystal for the Rush (frost token in green).
  - *Rush end:* Fizz hiccups one bubble.
  - *Stage-up:* the tube refills from the bomb end with a glug (the respool tint turns green).
  - *Last Spark:* the flask boils (bubbles burst on the bomb).
- **Why it is epic:** Fizz becomes a character who does something, not only a face that reacts.

#### Sandglass (u_sandglass) - Legendary fuse, Time Vault set
- **Look:** a long glass channel with gold sand flowing toward the bomb (`rope.flow`, the drift Plasma already uses). In Low the
  sand runs red-gold, and in Critical it runs thin. The burning end is a gold `star` flame with `grain` particles pouring down.
  **Fizz** is an hourglass-shaped bomb: two glass bulbs in a brass frame, its face on the upper bulb, sand in the lower one.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Rush start:* Fizz flips over (the body turns 180 degrees with a bounce while the face layer stays upright) and the sand
    stops. The rope's flow pauses under a gold-white frost: time has stopped.
  - *Rush end:* Fizz flips back with a clack.
  - *Stage-up:* Fizz flips once as the rope respools.
  - *Last Spark:* the last grains fall in slow motion. *Relit:* sand pours back up the channel.
- **Why it is epic:** the bomb is an hourglass, and it shows Rush and stage-ups the way the rules work.

#### Runechain (u_runechain) - Legendary fuse, a room of its own (the Undercroft)
- **Look:** a chain of carved slate runestones (18 px each, each with a glyph) linked by glowing cyan light. Behind the flame the
  stones go dark. In Low the links glow amber, in Critical red. The flame is the `spike` shape in cyan-white, with stone `chip` and
  glyph motes. **Fizz** is a little stone golem: a granite sphere with moss on top and glowing cyan rune cracks, with pale cyan eyes.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Rush start:* the golem awakens. The runes on its body blaze, a cyan shockwave ring leaves the bomb, and a shimmer runs along
    the chain.
  - *Rush end:* the golem yawns and settles.
  - *Stage-up:* a new rune is carved on Fizz's body: one more of eight glyphs lights per stage (8 static elements, opacity).
  - *Last Spark:* the golem's eyes close. *Relit:* they snap open.
- **Why it is epic:** Fizz becomes a creature that wakes for Rush and wears its run on its body.

#### Moonrise (u_moonrise) - Mythic fuse, Spire set
- **Look:** a silver ribbon of moonlight with stardust drifting along it (`rope.flow` plus `stardust` particles). Low and Critical
  use the shared warm tints. The flame is a cold white-blue `orb` with a halo ring, like a small moon. **Fizz is the moon:** a pale
  silver sphere with craters, and its phase shows the time left. It is full above 50 percent, gibbous from 25 to 50 percent, half
  under 25 percent, a thin crescent under 10 seconds, and a dark new moon with a bright rim at Last Spark. The phase is a shadow
  disc inside the clipped body that moves one step when the urgency state changes (one transform write per state, nothing per
  frame). The face is always drawn above the shadow.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Rush start:* full moon blaze. Fizz goes full and bright, a silver halo ring spreads from the bomb across the HUD, and stars
    twinkle along the rail. Fizz glows for the whole Rush (silver frost token).
  - *Last Spark:* a lunar eclipse. The moon goes dark, and the existing 2.5 s ring around the bomb is drawn as the eclipse's
    corona of fire.
  - *Relit:* moonrise. The moon flares back to full with a shower of stardust.
  - *Stage-up:* the moon waxes from crescent to full in 0.6 s as the rope respools.
  - *Line Blast:* a moonbeam runs from Fizz along the rope to the flame.
- **Why it is epic:** the bomb becomes a clock that can be read without looking at the number, and the eclipse makes the best
  Last Spark in the game.
- **Readability:** the phase is an extra cue that does not depend on colour, on top of the readout colour, the icon, the face and
  the sound. It never replaces them.

- **A pop:** the pane shatters into glass shards in its colour plus dark slivers of lead. Where the brick was, a spot of moonlight
  shines for a moment.
- **Signature moments:**
  - *Big pop (8+):* a shaft of moonlight falls on the group's place from the top of the board, with silver motes.
  - *Line Blast:* light streams through the line, as if a window had been opened (the beam turns silver).
  - *Rush:* the moon comes up behind the window. Every pane shows its own slice of one full moon (a crisp bright rim, dark seas and
    two craters), and the gaps glow silver. The whole board reads as one leaded window onto the full moon.
  - *Stage-up:* the moon's phase in the gaps grows one step.
- **Why it is epic:** a single picture spread across 64 moving bricks has never appeared in the game. It is the Pass's capstone.
- **Build:** as prototyped. `.face` holds the glass and lead lattice; `.stud` holds the etched sigil (Shape hints clip-paths);
  `.skin-moonglass::before` on the field holds the moon behind the bricks; in Rush, `.deco` draws the brick's slice of the moon
  with radial gradients positioned from `--r` and `--c` (engine addition 2). Nothing animates on the bricks: the moon is static
  paint, and Rush changes one class (one repaint of 64 bricks at Rush start).
- **Budget:** 0 brick animations; 1 opacity transition on the field layer.
- **Readability (measured):** Delta E 16.7 at rest and 12.9 in Rush on a seven-colour board (Lantern 11.9). The moon's light is
  capped at 20 percent white over the glass. Most of the moon's definition comes from the rim, the seas and the gaps, not from
  brightness, because a brighter moon washed Sapphire and Amethyst toward the same lilac (a first version measured 9.2).

## 6. Prototypes

All three were built outside the game, in `prototypes/`. `prototypes/shoot.js` loads the real game read-only, injects a prototype stylesheet
(and for Sigil a small script that builds the moment's DOM), swaps the field's skin class, and screenshots the real run screen at
393x852, 3x (iPhone 15 Pro). Frames of moments are posed by setting each element's transform for a time `t`. Readability is
measured from the same screenshots (6.4).

### 6.1 Hourglass (hardest idea 1: detailed art at 44 px, a 64-brick wave, a freeze)

- What was built: the full skin CSS as specified in 3.1 (`prototypes/hourglass.css`): the gold-framed colour panel, the glass-and-brass
  SVG, the coloured sand in two `scaleY` layers on every third brick, the sand stream, the Rush freeze, and the stage-up flip frames
  (rotation per brick from `(r + c) * 26 ms`).
- Result: at a cell size of 43.6 px (the board on a 393-wide phone) the hourglass reads clearly, the sand level is visible on every
  brick, and the five stage-1 colours and all seven colours stay distinct. The flip wave reads as one diagonal wave, as designed.
  Two changes came out of the review and are already in the spec: a darker glass interior so the sand stands out, and sand from

### 6.4 Readability, measured

The probe takes the mean colour of the centre 40 percent of every brick on a screenshot, then finds the smallest CIE76 Delta E between
two bricks of different colours, for normal vision and for simulated deuteranopia and protanopia (Machado 2009, severity 1). The
"seven colours" board is a real run board recoloured so every colour sits next to every other one (a late-stage board).

| Skin | 7 colours, rest | 7 colours, Rush | Deuteranopia (rest) | Weakest pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classic (shipped) | 27.0 | | 3.5 | Topaz/Amber, Sapphire/Amethyst (CVD) |
| Galaxy (shipped) | 22.6 | | 1.0 | Amber/Topaz |
| Neon (shipped) | 14.9 | | 1.8 | Amethyst/Sapphire |
| Lantern (shipped) | **11.9** (weakest shipped) | | 0.3 | Amber/Topaz |
| Hourglass (prototype) | 16.1 | 13.0 | 2.5 | Topaz/Amber |
| Moonglass (prototype) | 16.7 | 12.9 | 3.3 | Topaz/Amber, Sapphire/Amethyst in Rush |
| Sigil (prototype) | 19.5 | 19.1 | 1.8 | Topaz/Amber |

Reading it: the three prototypes clear the 12.5 bar in 3.0, and so stay above the weakest shipped skin (11.9), at rest and in Rush. Under colour-vision
deficiency, Sapphire and Amethyst are nearly the same colour on every skin, shipped or new, so colour alone can never separate them.
That is why the series carries the sigils: on Moonglass and Sigil the shape tells the colours apart even when the hue does not. A
seven-colour sheet of the three prototypes, at rest and in Rush:
`prototypes/sheet_seven_colours.jpg`.
The probe averages the middle of each brick, so on Moonglass and Sigil it includes the white sigil. It measures colour, not
whether a shape is recognised; shape recognition was judged by eye on the screenshots. The raw numbers are in
`prototypes/*_report.json`.

## 7. Risks and open questions for the owner

1. **Season track (5.4).** Series 4's Pass rewards and the returning-player economy depend on a Pass that resets per season.
   This is a separate piece of work for the Update 5 plan.
2. **The Hourglass flip** is the one place a skin animates more than a third of the board. It is allowed only during the stage-up
   hold, with a Smooth-level fallback, and it must be measured.
3. **Floating Isles** is the first light scene. If it cannot pass the HUD-text contrast test with a scrim, it moves to dusk colours.
4. **Moonglass in Rush** sits close to the readability bar (12.9 against 11.9). Its moon-light cap is a tested number and must not
   be raised for looks.
5. **Size and startup.** Bakes run on first use (3.6). The added CSS and JS should be weighed in the bundle and in startup time on a
   real iPhone.
6. **Old Pass exclusives** (Chrome, Prism Heart) for new players: the suggestion is trophies, which is the owner's call.
7. **Not verified:** real-phone frame times, Safari rendering of `color-mix()` with `var()` inside gradients on iOS 17 (Series 3
   already relies on it), and how the new pop sounds sound to a listener.
